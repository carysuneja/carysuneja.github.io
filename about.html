Introduction

Learning, leading, improving.

My professional journey

I’m a lifelong trainer, teacher, and improver of processes, teams, and organizations. The work I love best is solving problems that get in the way of fulfilling work—inefficient work streams, unclear communication or lines of authority, and individual skills in leading people or leading work. I work best when I know what the organization values and can tap into its formal and informal knowledge systems to bring better ways of working to the surface.

I spent my early career in manufacturing at PPG Industries in Oak Creek, Wisconsin; Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania; and Ft. Myers, Florida. PPG was more than 100 years old when I started, and I benefitted from its defined processes and deep expertise in its industries. With PPG, I was lucky enough to work on and lead projects that illustrated the connection between how work is organized and how people feel about the job, and to learn how to break a job down to its component parts. During my tenure as training manager at the Oak Creek plant, PPG won the Wisconsin Manufacturer of the Year Award twice for training initiatives.

I left the Oak Creek plant when I was promoted to Global Quality Training Manager. In that role, I was certified in Lean Manufacturing by the University of Michigan and traveled the world working with leaders and work teams grow skills and analyze processes. I launched online learning for policy deployment to PPG’s top 6,000 managers and was awarded a Training Magazine E-Learning Award for the instructional design in that program.

In Ft. Myers, I worked at a automotive replacement glass call center owned by PPG, where I was brought in to modernize their onboarding program. The call center had 400% turnover per year and astronomical training costs; a year later, new hire and just-in-time training were either partially or fully online, and several members of the training team had moved from classroom training to instructional design using training authoring tools. Costs—and turnover—went down as a result.

My interest in working with groups to remove hassles in their workstreams led me to a seven-year career in consulting with FlowOne, where I worked with clients in health care and deepened my knowledge of facilitation and the ways in which all work processes, regardless of industry, share certain critical elements. Now, with Fencing Supply Group, I’m able to put all of those skills to work as part of the small Service & Operations team. We act as internal consultants to the business, helping to evaluate how jobs are structured, the tools people need to do their work efficiently, and the operating systems that can align a group of acquired companies around a common experience and goal.
