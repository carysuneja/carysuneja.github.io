Introduction

Learning, leading, improving.

My professional journey

I’m a lifelong trainer, teacher, and improver of processes, teams, and organizations. The work I love best is solving problems that get in the way of fulfilling work—inefficient workflows, unclear communication or lines of authority, and gaps in the skills needed to lead people or manage work. I work best when I understand what an organization values and can tap into its formal and informal knowledge systems to bring better ways of working to the surface.

I spent my early career in manufacturing at PPG Industries in Oak Creek, Wisconsin; Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania; and Ft. Myers, Florida. PPG was more than 100 years old when I started, and I benefited from its well-defined processes and deep industry expertise. At PPG, I had the opportunity to work on and lead projects that illustrated the connection between how work is organized and how people feel about their jobs. I also learned how to break a job down into its component parts. During my tenure as training manager at the Oak Creek plant, PPG won the Wisconsin Manufacturer of the Year Award twice for training initiatives.

I left the Oak Creek plant when I was promoted to Global Quality Training Manager. In that role, I earned a certification in Lean Manufacturing from the University of Michigan and traveled the world, working with leaders and work teams to grow skills and analyze processes. I launched online learning for policy deployment to PPG’s top 6,000 managers and received a Training Magazine E-Learning Award for the instructional design of that program.

In Ft. Myers, I worked at an automotive replacement glass call center owned by PPG, where I was brought in to modernize its onboarding program. The call center had 400% turnover per year and astronomical training costs. A year later, new-hire and just-in-time training were either partially or fully online, and several members of the training team had transitioned from classroom training to instructional design using training authoring tools. Costs—and turnover—went down as a result.

My interest in helping groups remove hassles from their workflows led me to a seven-year consulting career with FlowOne. There, I worked with clients in health care and deepened my knowledge of facilitation and the critical elements that work processes share, regardless of industry. Now, at Fencing Supply Group, I put those skills to work as part of the small Service & Operations team. We act as internal consultants to the business, helping evaluate how jobs are structured, what tools people need to work efficiently, and which operating systems can align a group of acquired companies around a common experience and goal.
